The Puits d'Enfer: the spectacular chasm of the wild coast

South of Les Sables-d'Olonne, on the rocky coast of Château-d'Olonne, the Puits d'Enfer ("Hell's Well") is a natural chasm into which the ocean surges with a roar. It is one of the most impressive sites on the Vendée coast… and it lies right opposite our Les Reflets de la Loubine apartment, in the Lagrange L'Estran residence.

{{PHOTO:act-puits-enfer}}

What is the Puits d'Enfer?

The Puits d'Enfer is a narrow cleft cut into the cliff, into which the sea rushes. At high tide, waves shoot up in sprays of foam and the rumble of the water echoes through the rock. At low tide the scene is calmer, but you get a better sense of how deep the chasm is.

It is part of the wild coast (côte sauvage) of Château-d'Olonne, a rocky stretch of shoreline that contrasts with the long sandy beaches of Les Sables-d'Olonne.

When to come: high tide and rough seas

The best time is around high tide, especially during spring tides or on days with a westerly wind and a strong swell: that is when the chasm really "breathes". Check the tide times before you set off.

The site can be visited all year round. Autumn and winter often bring the most spectacular waves, and the sunset over the ocean is worth the trip on its own.

How to get there

From our apartment, the Puits d'Enfer is a few minutes' walk away: the residence is on rue du Puits d'Enfer, facing the site.

From the centre of Les Sables-d'Olonne, the nicest way is to follow the coast by bike along the cycle paths, or on foot along the coastal path. In summer, parking nearby can be difficult: walking and cycling remain the best options.

Take care near the edge

This is a natural site and the sea can be violent here. Keep well back from the edge, never climb down into the chasm and do not cross the barriers. The rocks are slippery and in rough weather a wave can catch you by surprise: keep children close and dogs on a lead.

Legends and history

Its name comes from the many legends surrounding it. In the best-known one, the devil himself split the cliff open to swallow a man who had come to make a pact with him; other tales speak of a fisherman punished for breaking a promise, whose moans can supposedly still be heard at the bottom of the chasm.

Reality has sometimes caught up with legend: in February 1949, the discovery of a trunk containing a man's body at the bottom of the Puits d'Enfer led to a criminal case that long left its mark on the area.

Nearby

The coastal path along the corniche links one ocean viewpoint to the next. Close by you will also find Cayola cove, a small inlet surrounded by rocks, and Tanchet beach. A little further on, the Romanesque Abbey of Saint-Jean-d'Orbestier recalls the rich history of Château-d'Olonne.
